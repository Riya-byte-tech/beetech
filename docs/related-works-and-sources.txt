# Related Works and Sources — Research Base

A research sweep across KVIC, NBB/NBHM, FSSAI, ICAR, government material,
honey-authentication research, IoT beekeeping research, blockchain
traceability literature, existing commercial systems, and traceability
standards.

**Key finding:** Honey Chain is not entering an empty space. India already
has pieces of the system — especially Madhukranti — while research and
commercial systems already cover smart hives. The opportunity is to
integrate these fragmented capabilities into one trustworthy, operational
ecosystem.

This document is organized into 10 research domains.

---

# 1. The government ecosystem

Two closely related "Honey Mission" ecosystems.

### A. KVIC Honey Mission — Ministry of MSME

KVIC's Honey Mission operates under the **Gramodyog Vikas Yojana**, with a
livelihood-development focus. The official GVY guideline states its
objectives as: beekeeping skill training, distribution of bee boxes with
live colonies, toolkit provision, handholding support, and training in
honey extraction and colony multiplication. The standard intervention
includes 10-day beekeeping skill development training, followed by
equipment/tool support. ([MSME][1])

KVIC has scaled this substantially. In May 2025, KVIC reported more than
229,000 bee boxes/colonies distributed, approximately 20,000 tonnes of
honey produced, and around ₹325 crore income generated for beekeepers.
([Press Information Bureau][2])

This is not merely historical activity — Honey Mission training has continued
through 2026, per KVIC's ongoing progress reporting. ([Press Information
Bureau][3])

### B. National Beekeeping & Honey Mission — NBB / Ministry of Agriculture

India separately has the **National Beekeeping & Honey Mission (NBHM)**,
implemented through the National Bee Board, with three Mini Missions:

| Mission | Focus                                                        |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| MM-I    | Production & productivity                                     |
| MM-II   | Collection, processing, storage, marketing, value addition    |
| MM-III  | Research & technology                                         |

NBB explicitly lists blockchain/traceability, IT tools, quality-control
labs, disease diagnostic facilities, honey processing, marketing
infrastructure, Honey Corridors and FPOs among NBHM objectives.
([NBB Recruit][4]) ([Press Information Bureau][5])

PS 26021 sits at the intersection of KVIC/MSME, NBB/Agriculture,
FSSAI/Food Safety, beekeepers/FPOs/processors/traders, and consumers —
which is why the PS is so broad.

---

# 2. Madhukranti already exists

The single most important discovery of this research pass: India already
has **Madhukranti**, a government-backed portal operated under NBB for
honey-source traceability.

[Madhukranti Portal](https://madhukranti.in/nbb/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The portal provides: beekeeper registration, aggregator/trader
registration, processor registration, certificate issuance, certificate
renewal, certificate verification, bee-colony related registration,
eMarket functionality, training resources, and disease-management
resources. ([Madhukranti][6])

Its stated purpose is explicitly "Traceability of Source of Honey and Other
Hive Products." It asks beekeepers and businesses involved in production,
processing, packaging, marketing and exporting to register and provide
authentic business information. ([Madhukranti][6])

The portal states its collected data is intended for traceability,
government/regulatory agencies, welfare schemes, training, and
standards/regulations, and that registered users can view their entered
information through authenticated access. ([Madhukranti][6])

The problem is therefore not "India has no honey traceability system" —
that framing is factually weak. The accurate framing:

> **India has an existing digital registration/traceability ecosystem, but
> PS 26021 calls for a more integrated system combining secure batch
> traceability, consumer verification, IoT hive monitoring, AI analytics
> and scalable cluster deployment.**

That is a substantially stronger problem framing.

# 4. Government infrastructure is already extensive

The NBHM 2025 progress report lists, as sanctioned under NBHM up to March
2025: 6 world-class honey testing labs, 47 mini honey testing labs, 6
disease diagnostic labs, 8 custom hiring centres, 26 honey processing
units, 12 beekeeping equipment units, 18 collection/branding/marketing
units, and 10 packaging/cold-storage facilities. ([Press Information
Bureau][5])

It also reports 424 hectares of technology demonstration, 288 hectares of
bee-friendly flora, 167 women-empowerment activities, a National Centre of
Excellence in Beekeeping at IIT Roorkee, and 97 beekeeper/honey-producer
FPOs formed under the relevant NBHM allocation. ([Press Information
Bureau][5])

This changes the deployment framing: Honey Chain does not need to invent a
laboratory network or a government honey ecosystem — it can become a
digital coordination and trust layer over existing infrastructure.

# 5. Modeling the traceability chain

GS1's Global Traceability Standard defines traceability around origin →
processing → distribution → location/history, emphasizing identification of
the relevant objects, parties, locations and events throughout the supply
chain. ([GS1][7]) This is a stronger conceptual model than simply storing
"batch information" — the system should model events:

```text
EVENT 01
Hive registered
        ↓
EVENT 02
Hive assigned to beekeeper
        ↓
EVENT 03
Hive moved to floral location
        ↓
EVENT 04
Honey harvested
        ↓
EVENT 05
Batch created
        ↓
EVENT 06
Batch transported
        ↓
EVENT 07
Processing
        ↓
EVENT 08
Quality test
        ↓
EVENT 09
Packaging
        ↓
EVENT 10
QR activated
        ↓
EVENT 11
Product sold
```

This is much closer to a serious traceability architecture.

# 6. What blockchain research actually offers

The research literature is broadly positive about blockchain's usefulness
for food traceability. A 2024 systematic review identifies immutability and
automation as particularly important blockchain characteristics for
agri-food traceability. ([ScienceDirect][8]) Another 2024 survey identifies
lack of standardization and participation as major barriers to agricultural
blockchain traceability. ([ScienceDirect][9])

This matters directly: **blockchain does not solve bad data.** If someone
enters "100% pure honey" without evidence, blockchain makes that false
statement permanently recorded:

```text
Bad input
   ↓
Blockchain
   ↓
Permanent bad data
```

What's actually needed:

```text
Data
 ↓
Validation
 ↓
Authorized event
 ↓
Evidence
 ↓
Blockchain
```

This distinction is one of the project's central technical arguments.

---

# 7. Existing blockchain food systems already prove the QR concept

IBM Food Trust uses blockchain-based shared traceability across food supply
chains. ([IBM][10]) A particularly relevant example is Pietro Coricelli's
olive oil system, where consumers scan a QR code on the bottle to access
information from an immutable ledger, including product history and
analyses/tests. ([IBM Newsroom][11]) That is close to the consumer
interaction proposed here:

```text
Physical product
      ↓
     QR
      ↓
Digital product identity
      ↓
Traceability record
      ↓
Evidence
```

QR + blockchain is not novel by itself and should not be claimed as such.
The novelty needs to come from the honey-specific integration.

---

# 8. Prior art: a blockchain "Honey Chain" already exists

A 2025 research paper, **"Fujairah Honey Chain (FHC): A Blockchain
Framework for Monitoring Honey Production,"** is directly relevant. It
proposes a blockchain framework for honey trade combining blockchain, IoT,
quality/origin verification, oracle mechanisms, smart contracts, batch
tracking, and tokens. It specifically addresses the oracle problem — how to
know that data entering blockchain is actually trustworthy. ([MDPI][12])

This means the claim "no one has combined blockchain and honey" is false —
it has been done. The real research question becomes:

> **What does Honey Chain need to do differently for India's KVIC/NBB rural
> beekeeping ecosystem?**

A more interesting and more defensible research gap.

# 9. Smart beekeeping technology already exists

### Beemon

A research system that continuously monitors temperature, humidity,
weight, audio, and video, sending information through MQTT to a dashboard.
([ScienceDirect][13])

### Multiparametric hive-sensing system

Research combining hive weight, acoustic signals, temperature, humidity,
and CO₂ using Raspberry Pi and sensors. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][14])

### BeeLive

A research deployment monitoring 28 hives using temperature, humidity,
microphones, cameras, scales, and ThingsBoard, collecting continuous hive
data. ([ScienceDirect][15])

IoT hive monitoring itself is not novel.

NBB's advisory states beekeepers should periodically inspect colonies for

# 12. Disease detection is more complicated than it first appears

NBB's official disease material lists significant honeybee problems:

**Pests:** greater wax moth, lesser wax moth, Varroa mite, Tropilaelaps
mite, tracheal mite, wasps/hornets, and other predators.

**Diseases:** American foulbrood, European foulbrood, chalkbrood, sacbrood,
nosemosis.

NBB's advisory states that abnormality/disease should be properly
diagnosed, ideally with expert/research/government assistance — which
means the claim "our AI diagnoses bee diseases" is too strong. A more
defensible architecture is **AI-assisted hive health and disease-risk
detection**, with outputs such as:

```text
Normal
↓
Possible anomaly
↓
Elevated disease risk
↓
Recommend inspection
↓
Expert confirmation
```

This respects the actual agricultural workflow.

# 13. AI disease detection research is advancing quickly

A 2026 study developed a multimodal system for detecting Varroa destructor
using computer vision, acoustic analysis, and environmental sensing on an
edge-computing platform. ([ScienceDirect][19]) Another 2026 study used deep
learning on bee acoustic spectrograms, demonstrating strong classification
performance under natural environmental noise. ([ScienceDirect][20])

```text
                 Hive
                  │
       ┌──────────┼──────────┐
       ↓          ↓          ↓
   Environment  Audio      Vision
       │          │          │
       └──────────┼──────────┘
                  ↓
             AI Fusion
                  ↓
          Colony Health Score
                  ↓
          Disease Risk Alert
```

This is more scientifically defensible than claiming a single temperature
sensor can diagnose disease.

# 14. Productivity prediction is a legitimate but under-researched area

A January 2026 systematic review on machine learning for predicting bee
honey production and quality reviewed 105 studies, of which only 11
addressed honey yield, compared with 94 focused on honey quality —
indicating a genuine research gap. ([ScienceDirect][21]) For honey yield
prediction, the literature commonly uses hive sensors, hive weight, climate
records, and environmental conditions, with ensemble/boosting models
frequently used. ([ScienceDirect][21]) A 2026 Scientific Reports paper also
demonstrates ML-based honey-yield prediction combined with environmental
risk assessment. ([Nature][22]) This component of PS 26021 has genuine
research grounding.

# 15. Honey authenticity is a distinct AI problem

There are two kinds of "authenticity":

### A. Provenance authenticity

Did this honey actually come from the claimed beekeeper/batch/location?
Addressed by blockchain + traceability.

### B. Chemical authenticity

Is this honey actually pure, or has it been adulterated? Addressed by
laboratory analysis, spectroscopy, and ML. These are not the same problem.

---

# 16. FSSAI honey-quality requirements

FSSAI standards specify parameters including moisture, fructose/glucose
ratio, sucrose, acidity, HMF, diastase activity, C4 sugars, pollen count,
foreign oligosaccharides, proline, electrical conductivity, and
carbon-isotope criteria. The FSSAI compendium specifies, among other
limits, HMF ≤80 mg/kg and C4 sugar ≤7%. ([FSSAI][23])

FSSAI also publishes an official Manual of Methods of Analysis for Honey
and Other Bee Hive Products, including isotope-ratio analysis for C4 sugar
and related parameters. ([FSSAI][24]) In November 2025, FSSAI clarified
that honey exceeding the 80 mg/kg HMF limit is categorized as substandard,
treating HMF as a quality parameter rather than automatically unsafe.
([FSSAI][25])

# 17. A defensible Honey Chain authenticity architecture

Rather than "AI tells you whether honey is real":

```text
LAB TEST
   │
   ├── C4 sugar
   ├── HMF
   ├── isotopic analysis
   ├── foreign oligosaccharides
   └── other parameters
          │
          ▼
     TEST REPORT
          │
          ▼
      HASH / ID
          │
          ▼
      BLOCKCHAIN
          │
          ▼
       QR CODE
          │
          ▼
      CONSUMER
```

The blockchain proves the certificate belongs to this batch and hasn't been
altered. The laboratory proves the sample met or didn't meet the relevant
quality criteria. That is a far more credible trust architecture.

# 18. AI can also assist chemical authenticity

### FTIR + ML

Research demonstrates FTIR spectroscopy combined with machine learning can
detect honey adulteration. A 2023 study detected rice/corn syrup
adulteration using FTIR and chemometrics with 96% specificity for tested
adulteration levels. ([ScienceDirect][26]) Another study used ATR-FTIR and
machine learning to identify rice-syrup adulteration with high accuracy.
([ScienceDirect][27]) A 2025 Indian study investigated NIR spectroscopy +
chemometrics for detecting sugar syrup, jaggery syrup, rice syrup, and corn
syrup in Indian honey samples. ([ScienceDirect][28]) A 2026 study
demonstrated ML-assisted FTIR detection of glucose/rice-syrup adulteration
with very high classification performance under controlled experimental
conditions. ([ScienceDirect][29])

The major caveat: these models require properly collected spectral
datasets. Training "pure vs fake honey" on random honey photos would be
scientifically weak.

---

# 19. A caution from Indian honey-authentication research

A 2025 study of Indian honey samples using carbon stable-isotope criteria
found that 94% met several major criteria including C4 sugar-related
criteria, 86% met the foreign-oligosaccharide criterion, but a surprisingly
high proportion showed issues against one Δδ¹³C criterion. The authors
discuss bee feeding practices as a possible explanation and call for
further investigation. ([MDPI][30])

This is directly relevant: authenticity is not always as simple as "C4 >
7% → fake." Biological production practices can complicate interpretation.
**Honey Chain should present evidence + confidence + test provenance, not
pretend to be a magical truth detector.**

---

# 20. What the research says about honey fraud

The literature identifies several categories:

**Direct adulteration:** adding sugar syrup, corn syrup, rice syrup, glucose
syrup, or other inexpensive sweeteners.

**Indirect adulteration:** feeding bees exogenous sugars under certain
practices.

**Mislabeling:** claiming incorrect floral origin, incorrect geographical
origin, monofloral honey when it isn't, or misleading quality claims.

Research confirms that conventional physicochemical measurements alone may
not reliably identify all adulteration types. ([ScienceDirect][31]) This is
why the authenticity story should be traceability + laboratory evidence +
analytics, rather than blockchain alone.

# 21. The oracle problem

Raised directly by the Fujairah Honey Chain research. If a beekeeper
enters:

```text
Temperature = 34°C
Honey = 100% pure
Location = X
Yield = 25kg
```

blockchain cannot know whether those numbers are true.

```text
Real world
    ↓
Oracle / data ingestion
    ↓
Validation
    ↓
Blockchain
```

The oracle layer is critical. For Honey Chain, possible trusted inputs:

**Sensor oracle:** ESP32 → gateway → server → validated telemetry.

**Laboratory oracle:** lab system → signed certificate → blockchain hash.

**Institutional oracle:** authorized NBB/KVIC officer → verified event.

**GPS/location oracle:** device → location → movement event.

This gives a strong technical research direction.

# 23. Rural deployment changes the architecture

A rural beekeeper may lack continuous internet, expensive smartphones,
constant electricity, reliable cellular coverage, or technical expertise.
The system should be offline-first:

```text
Hive / Mobile
      ↓
Local storage
      ↓
Internet available?
   ↙          ↘
 YES          NO
  ↓            ↓
Sync       Queue events
             ↓
         Later sync
```

The IoT device should not require every sensor to have direct internet
connectivity. A more realistic architecture:

```text
10–20 Hives
     ↓
Local low-power communication
     ↓
One Gateway
     ↓
4G/Wi-Fi when available
     ↓
Cloud
```

Consistent with commercial systems such as BeeHero, which use local hive
sensors communicating with a gateway before cloud transmission.
([BeeHero][32])

# 28. The key research insight

The winning solution should not be marketed as "a blockchain-based honey
tracking app" (too generic), "an AI disease detection app" (too narrow),
"an IoT smart hive" (already exists), or "a QR code for honey" (already
commonplace). The stronger proposition:

> **A digital trust and intelligence layer connecting the hive, beekeeper,
> honey batch, laboratory evidence, supply chain and consumer — designed
> specifically for India's rural beekeeping ecosystem.**

---

# 29. Research gaps requiring further investigation

### Priority 1 — Existing government workflows

Understand Madhukranti registration, certificate generation, beekeeper IDs,
bee-colony registration, processor registration, aggregator workflow,
verification workflow, Honey MIS, KVIC beneficiary workflow, FPO workflow,
and honey testing workflow — specifically what information is already
collected, to avoid duplicating government data.

### Priority 2 — The actual honey supply chain

Map bee colony → beekeeper → apiary → harvest → collection → aggregation →
processing → testing → bottling → distributor → retailer → consumer,
including ownership transfer, batch splitting, batch mixing, repackaging,
recalls, failed quality tests, and returns. This determines the blockchain
data model.

### Priority 3 — Honey authenticity

Compare FSSAI methods, NMR, FTIR, NIR, Raman, IRMS, HMF, C4 sugar, foreign
oligosaccharides, pollen analysis, and botanical-origin classification, and
decide what can realistically be demonstrated in an SIH prototype.

### Priority 4 — Smart hive AI

Determine what data can support colony-health scoring, anomaly detection,
Varroa risk, swarming prediction, absconding prediction, disease risk, and
honey-yield prediction — and which datasets actually exist.

### Priority 5 — IoT economics

Investigate ESP32, LoRa/LoRaWAN, BLE, GSM, Wi-Fi, sensor cost, battery
life, solar power, gateway cost, rural connectivity, sensor placement,
calibration, and weatherproofing — "six sensors per hive" is very different
from something KVIC could realistically deploy.

### Priority 6 — Blockchain architecture

Compare Hyperledger Fabric, Polygon, Ethereum, private Ethereum, Besu,
Avalanche, and other permissioned DLTs, and answer why blockchain at all,
what exactly goes on-chain, and who operates the nodes.

### Priority 7 — Existing competitors

Benchmark BeeHero, GoBuzzr, BroodMinder, Beemon, BeeLive, Fujairah Honey
Chain, Madhukranti, IBM Food Trust, and other agricultural traceability
systems — not just features, but architecture, limitations, pricing/
business model, and deployment assumptions.

## The research question this leads to

Rather than "how do we build Honey Chain," the sharper question:

> ### **"How can we integrate India's existing beekeeper registration, hive
> management, honey testing and market infrastructure into a trusted,
> event-based digital ecosystem that adds real-time hive intelligence and
> consumer-verifiable provenance?"**

This gives a much richer basis for the architecture, literature review,
novelty argument, and SIH pitch.

### Next research pass

The recommended next step is a deeper pass into the actual workflows and
data: Madhukranti → KVIC Honey Mission → Honey MIS → beekeeper → hive →
harvest → processing → laboratory → packaging → QR → consumer → FPO/market
