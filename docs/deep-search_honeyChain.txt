# Honey Chain — Workflow & Data Research

This document reconstructs the real Honey Chain workflow from institutional
evidence, rather than designing it from the SIH description alone. The
central finding: the ecosystem already has a concrete institutional
structure. **Honey Chain should therefore be designed as an integration
layer, not as a replacement for KVIC/NBB/FSSAI systems.**

# 1. Government architecture

The **National Beekeeping & Honey Mission (NBHM)** is not a single
department. Its 2020 operational guidelines establish a multi-level
structure involving the National Bee Board, state agriculture/horticulture
departments, district committees, KVIC, ICAR, SAUs/CAUs, FPOs,
cooperatives, NAFED, NDDB, TRIFED, food-processing agencies, FSSAI, APEDA,
and others. The national-level steering structure included representatives
from MSME, FSSAI, APEDA, KVIC, ICAR, MoFPI, Rural Development, Tribal
Affairs and others.

**Honey Chain is inherently a multi-stakeholder system.** There is no
single "Honey Authority" performing every function.

---

# 2. The three NBHM layers

### MM-I — Production & productivity

Scientific beekeeping, bee colonies, bee breeders, training, disease
diagnostics, beekeeper registration, traceability/blockchain, technology
adoption, cluster development.

### MM-II — Post-harvest

Collection, processing, storage, marketing, value addition, processing
plants, testing facilities, branding, export.

### MM-III — Research & technology

Advanced technology, region-specific research, technology generation.

This maps directly onto the proposed system:

```text
MM-I
  ↓
Hive + Beekeeper + AI/IoT

MM-II
  ↓
Harvest + Batch + Processing + Testing + Market

MM-III
  ↓
Analytics + AI + Research + Technology
```

# 3. Beekeeper onboarding

KVIC's Honey Mission is built around training, distribution of live
colonies and bee boxes, toolkits, and technical handholding. The current
KVIC guideline describes 10-day skill-development training, normally 20
candidates per batch, followed by practical support for colony
multiplication and honey extraction. Each beneficiary is provided 10 bee
boxes with live colonies, a toolkit and foundation sheets under the
specified assistance structure. ([KVI Online][1])

A logical Honey Chain onboarding flow:

```text
Potential beekeeper
       ↓
Training
       ↓
KVIC / implementing agency
       ↓
Bee boxes + colonies
       ↓
Hive registration
       ↓
NBB/Madhukranti registration
       ↓
Active beekeeper
```

### Minimum beekeeper data

```text
Beekeeper
────────────
beekeeper_id
name
contact
address
district
state
training_status
training_certificate
experience
organization/FPO
NBB_registration_id
KVIC_beneficiary_id
```

Some of these fields are proposed integration fields rather than fields
currently exposed by every government system, and that distinction needs to
be maintained carefully.

# 4. Madhukranti — the existing digital backbone

Madhukranti was created specifically for "traceability of the source of
honey and other beehive products." Government material describes the
intended database as covering stakeholders involved in honey production,
sales, marketing, hive production, and other parts of the value chain.
([Press Information Bureau][2])

The portal launched on 7 April 2021, with Indian Bank as the
technical/banking partner. ([Press Information Bureau][2]) As of 14 October
2025, approximately 14,859 beekeepers, 269 beekeeping/honey societies, 150
firms, and 206 companies were registered with NBB on Madhukranti. ([Press
Information Bureau][3])

---

# 5. What Madhukranti captures

Government material describes the portal as intended to create a database
of stakeholders involved in honey and other hive-product production, sales
and marketing, with the planned system also capturing honey sales
transactions through a mobile application. ([IAS Gyan][4])

```text
Madhukranti
     │
     ├── Beekeeper
     ├── Society
     ├── Firm
     ├── Company
     ├── Producer
     ├── Trader
     ├── Processor
     └── Sales
```

**Honey Chain should not invent a parallel beekeeper registry if it can
integrate with this identity layer.**

---

# 6. Observed beekeeper registration fields

A real-world implementation of Madhukranti registration in Haryana exposes
the following fields:

**Personal:** Family/PPP ID, name, DOB, photograph, gender, PIN code,
mobile, email, district, block, village, father/husband name, category,
qualification, beekeeping experience.

**Beekeeping:** number of bee colonies, intention to increase colonies,
FPO/cooperative/SHG membership.

**Business:** business address.

**Nominee:** nominee name, DOB, relationship.

([Horticulture Department Haryana][5])

This is a state implementation linked to Madhukranti, and should be treated
as an observable implementation example rather than the definitive national
Madhukranti schema — it is nonetheless a strong indication of the master
data to accommodate.

# 7. Hive/colony identity

The Haryana Madhukranti-linked system requires individual bee boxes to have
identification, serial numbers, and traceability records, and requires
verified bee boxes for eligibility under its honey scheme. ([Horticulture
Department Haryana][5]) Older Madhukranti material also contains an
explicit bee-colony verification form, recording applicant, address,
mobile, online application number, colonies applied for, colonies found,
verification officer, officer contact, verification date, and signatures.
([Scribd][6])

This establishes `Hive / Colony` as a distinct entity — not "beekeeper owns
20 colonies" but:

```text
Beekeeper
   │
   ├── Hive H001
   ├── Hive H002
   ├── Hive H003
   ├── ...
   └── Hive H020
```

Each hive has its own lifecycle.

# 9. Apiary as a distinct entity

Beekeeper, apiary/location, and hive are distinct entities:

```text
Beekeeper P001

      ├── Apiary A001
      │     ├── Hive H001
      │     ├── Hive H002
      │     └── Hive H003
      │
      └── Apiary A002
            ├── Hive H004
            ├── Hive H005
            └── Hive H006
```

Bees may be moved between floral sources, so **location should be an event,
not just a static hive field.**

# 10. Hive migration

NBB has historically recognized the need to facilitate movement/migration
of bee colonies to bee-friendly locations. ([Scribd][8])

```text
Hive H001

Location A
   ↓
Migration event
   ↓
Location B
   ↓
Migration event
   ↓
Location C
```

The blockchain should not simply store `current_location = C` — it should
preserve the movement itself:

```text
MOVED
H001
A → B
date/time
authorized_by
reason
```

That is genuine traceability.

# 11. IoT belongs to the hive, not the batch

```text
Hive
 │
 └── Sensor Node
       │
       ├── Temperature
       ├── Humidity
       ├── Weight
       ├── Acoustic
       └── Battery
```

```text

 ↓
Gateway
 ↓

 ↓

```

A key data-model distinction: **sensor observation ≠ blockchain event.** A
hive might report temperature/humidity/weight every five minutes — this

```text

      ↓

      ↓

      ↓

      ↓

      ↓

```

# 12. What AI should consume

NBB disease material makes clear that colony problems are affected by
season, weather, colony strength, food availability, hygiene, queen
condition, parasites, disease, and environmental conditions. An AI system
should therefore not depend on a single sensor:

```text
Hive H001
─────────────
Temperature trend
Humidity trend
Weight trend
Acoustic features
Weather
Season
Location
Species
Historical health
Historical yield
Inspection records
```

```text
                  AI
                   │
          ┌────────┼─────────┐
          ↓        ↓         ↓
     Health      Disease   Yield
      Score       Risk    Prediction
```

# 13. AI disease output should not assert "disease confirmed"

Official NBB material describes field inspection and diagnosis for
diseases, recommending inspection/isolation/appropriate control measures —
disease identification generally involves visual symptoms and inspection
rather than sensor telemetry alone. AI output should therefore be phrased
as:

```text
🟢 Normal

🟡 Anomaly detected

🟠 Elevated disease risk

🔴 Critical — inspection recommended
```

rather than "Hive H001 has American foulbrood," unless an appropriate
diagnostic process has confirmed it. This distinction makes the solution
considerably more credible to an agricultural expert.

# 15. Batch genealogy

A batch can be split, mixed, processed, or packaged:

### Split

```text
Batch B001 — 100 kg
        │
   ┌────┴────┐
   ↓         ↓
 B001-A    B001-B
 60 kg      40 kg
```

### Mixed

```text
B001 — 40 kg
B002 — 60 kg
       ↓
     B003
    100 kg
```

### Processed

```text
Raw B001
   ↓
Processed B001-P
```

### Packaged

```text
Processed B001-P
        ↓
 ┌──────┼──────┐
 ↓      ↓      ↓
Jar1   Jar2   Jar3
```

This is why event-based traceability is more powerful than a simple `batch`
table. GS1's traceability model formalizes this using Critical Tracking
Events (CTEs) and Key Data Elements (KDEs). CTEs include events such as
harvesting, transformation, shipping, receiving and selling; KDEs describe
who/what/where/when/why. ([GS1][9])

---

# 16. Adopting the CTE/KDE model

| CTE               | Honey Chain interpretation |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| Beginning of life  | Honey batch created            |
| Transformation     | Processing                       |
| Shipping           | Batch dispatched                   |
| Receiving          | Processor/buyer receives             |
| Packaging          | Consumer product created               |
| Sale               | Product sold                             |
| Disposal           | Recalled/rejected product                  |

Each event carries KDEs: WHO, WHAT, WHERE, WHEN, WHY. GS1 recommends
uniquely identifying the object, parties and locations, and associating
events with time and location. ([GS1][10]) This is a strong foundation for
the blockchain schema.

# 17. Processing

Official NBHM guidelines describe honey processing as involving moisture
reduction, cleaning, wax separation, preparation for sale, and quality
management. Honey harvested under Indian conditions can have high moisture,
and processing may be required to bring it to the applicable standard; the
guidelines explicitly connect processing facilities to beekeepers,
organizations and FPOs.

```text
Raw honey
    ↓
Received at processor
    ↓
Processing
    ↓
Processed honey
```

must preserve:

```text
input_batch
processing_unit
operator
date/time
quantity_in
quantity_out
process_type
```

# 18. Laboratory testing

FSSAI maintains an official Manual of Methods of Analysis for Honey and
Other Beehive Products, covering methods for specific gravity, moisture,
reducing sugars, sucrose, fructose/glucose ratio, ash, acidity, HMF,
diastase, water-insoluble matter, pollen/plant elements, proline,
electrical conductivity, 2-AFGP as a rice-syrup marker, C4 sugar,
isotope-ratio parameters, foreign oligosaccharides, and other bee products
— a substantial basis for the data model.

# 20. The laboratory as a trusted actor

FSSAI's ecosystem already includes Food Business Operators, Food Analysts,
food laboratories, sampling processes, and laboratory reports. FoSCoS
provides systems for FBO licensing and registration, application tracking,
laboratory-related workflows and authority functions. ([Foscos][12])

The system should not allow "beekeeper → 'Lab test passed' → blockchain"
without an authoritative actor:

```text
Sample
  ↓
Authorized laboratory
  ↓
Test
  ↓
Signed result
  ↓
Certificate
  ↓
Certificate hash
  ↓
Blockchain
```

A substantially stronger trust model.

# 21. Packaging

FSSAI's labelling framework requires information including name of food,
FSSAI information, net quantity, lot/batch identification, date marking,
manufacturer/packer/brand-owner information, and other prescribed
declarations. A lot/code/batch number is explicitly the identification mark
by which food can be traced through manufacture and distribution.
([FSSAI][13])

The QR code should not replace the batch number — it should be linked to
it:

```text
Physical label

Batch No: HC-MH-2026-00127

QR
 ↓
Honey Chain digital identity
```

# 26. Failure cases

### Failure 1 — Fake beekeeper

Someone claims "I have 100 colonies" without basis.

**Solution:** registration → physical/authority verification → verified
colony count → identity activated. The Madhukranti colony-verification
concept directly supports this. ([Scribd][6])

### Failure 2 — Sensor lies

Sensor reports 100 kg honey but actual hive weight is 30 kg.

**Solution:** sensor calibration, anomaly detection, and periodic physical
verification. Blockchain cannot solve faulty sensors.

### Failure 3 — Fabricated harvest

"Hive H001 produced 50 kg," unverified.

**Solution:** require harvest evidence — hive association, timestamp,
quantity, optional sensor corroboration, beekeeper declaration, later
processing receipt. Confidence increases when independent evidence agrees.

### Failure 4 — Batch mixing

`B001 + B002 → B003`.

**Solution:** record transformation genealogy explicitly (`B003` has inputs
`B001` and `B002`).

### Failure 5 — Failed lab test

A failed batch must not become "Verified ✓" — it should enter a
`QUARANTINED` state, blocking downstream packaging.

### Failure 6 — Product recall

Blockchain genealogy allows the affected descendants of a recalled batch to
be identified — one of the strongest arguments for event-based
traceability.

# 32. Honey MIS

KVIC operates a live Honey MIS application, described by the official KVIC
application portal as the "KVIC Honey Mission Information System — manage
honey production, distribution and reporting." ([KVIC Apps][15]) An
observable page from the system contains actual production/bottling
inventory fields such as date, package size, opening balance, bottled
honey received from production/bottling unit, total bottled honey, damaged
bottles, honey issued to sales counter, and closing stock. ([KVIC Apps][16])

This proves an existing KVIC digital system already models part of:

```text
Production
   ↓
Bottling
   ↓
Inventory
   ↓
Sales counter
```

Honey Chain should connect the upstream provenance to these downstream
inventory processes rather than duplicating them.

# 36. Where the novelty lies

This research does not support claiming to have invented blockchain honey
traceability, smart hive monitoring, QR-based authentication, or AI disease
detection — these already exist in different forms. The defensible novelty:

> **An India-specific, interoperable digital ecosystem that links
> government-verified beekeeper/hive identities to harvest-level honey
> provenance, laboratory evidence, AI-assisted hive intelligence and
> consumer-verifiable product genealogy.**

Specifically:

**1. Government-aware** — designed around the KVIC + NBB/Madhukranti +
FSSAI + FPO ecosystem.

**2. Hive-to-batch linkage** — not "this beekeeper produced honey" but
"this batch originated from these verified hives during this harvest
event."

**3. Evidence-backed trust** — government verification + IoT evidence + lab
evidence + supply-chain events → blockchain proof.

**4. AI as recommendation, not authority** — risk/prediction/recommendation
outputs, with authoritative actors performing verification.

**5. Rural deployment** — cluster-based rather than requiring every
beekeeper to independently operate a sophisticated cloud system.

# 37. Surveillance and disaster management

NBHM guidelines state that digitization and traceability should also
support bee surveillance and disaster management — a scope larger than
honey alone:

```text
1,000 hives
      ↓
Honey Chain
      ↓
Regional dashboard
      ↓
Disease anomalies
      ↓
Cluster-level outbreak
      ↓
Authorities alerted
```

or:

```text
Regional weather event
       ↓
Affected apiaries
       ↓
Hive health deterioration
       ↓
Risk map
       ↓
Targeted intervention
```

The system can evolve from "track my honey" into "understand the health
and productivity of the beekeeping ecosystem" — aligned directly with
NBHM's stated objectives around data, surveillance, and scientific
beekeeping.

## Next steps

The recommended next step is turning this research into a formal system
specification, via four artifacts:

**A. Actor × Permission Matrix** — exactly what beekeeper / KVIC / NBB /
FPO / processor / lab / admin / consumer can create, read, verify, approve
and modify.

**B. Complete Event Dictionary** — for every event: actor, input, output,
required fields, evidence, validation, on-chain/off-chain status, and
failure states.

**C. Complete Entity/Data Dictionary** — every field, datatype,
relationship, and source for each entity (Beekeeper, Hive, Apiary, Harvest,
Batch, Processing, Lab, Certificate, Package, QR, ...).

**D. State Machines** — particularly the hive lifecycle
(`REGISTERED → VERIFIED → ACTIVE → MOVED → INSPECTION → RETIRED`) and the
honey batch lifecycle:

```text
CREATED → RECEIVED → PROCESSED → TESTING
                         │
                  ┌──────┴──────┐
                 PASS           FAIL
                  ↓               ↓
              PACKAGED        QUARANTINED
                  ↓
                SOLD
                  ↓
              CONSUMED
```

With those four in place, the blockchain architecture, database schema,
APIs, IoT architecture, AI pipeline and UI follow directly — and the answer
to "what does Honey Chain add over Madhukranti + Honey MIS" (one of the
most important questions an SIH evaluator can ask) becomes concrete.
